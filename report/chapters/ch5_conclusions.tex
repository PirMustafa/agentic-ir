\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Summary of Contributions}

This project set out to test a narrow claim: that adding one backward edge to
a retrieval-augmented pipeline changes what the system can do, and that the
change can be measured rather than described. What was built is a four-agent
system coordinated by an explicit state machine, covering all five tasks of
the brief's Table~1 and running on one consumer GPU with no paid API. The
Planner emits a validated graph of sub-queries. The Retrieval Agent routes each
to BM25, dense or hybrid search, fuses formulations with reciprocal rank
fusion~\cite{cormack2009reciprocal} and reranks only when the margin is
narrow. The KG Navigator links entities by alias and runs bidirectional search
over a graph induced from corpus text. The Verifier resolves citations, scores
entailment, and below its threshold returns a directive to the Planner instead
of an answer.

Three properties were established during construction rather than by the
benchmark. Schema-constrained planning with a local 8B model is reliable on
structure, 8 of 8 decompositions correctly shaped with no retries, and
unreliable on self-description, 7 of 8 strategy labels wrong, which is why
strategy is derived from the graph and the model's label discarded. Agency is
affordable here only because most of it is deterministic. And, the finding
this report did not expect to make, the model's reasoning mode, suppressed
everywhere for the grid, is worth more than any component of the architecture
when it is switched on for one agent.

\section{Empirical Findings}

The evaluation answers four questions, each of which had a defensible negative
answer, and the protocol was fixed before any was resolved.

\textbf{Does decomposition beat single-shot retrieval?} No, and it is a
significant no on one of two datasets. Against \texttt{self\_ask}, the
strongest baseline that answers, \texttt{agentic\_full} is $-0.093$ F1
$[-0.145, -0.039]$ on HotpotQA and $-0.043$ $[-0.100, +0.014]$ on
2WikiMultihopQA. On evidence the answer is different in kind: scored the same
way for every row, on the recall of the whole pool, the agentic system leads
every baseline significantly, and its citations resolve to real sentences at
0.960 and 0.976 where no baseline's resolve at all. Decomposition improves
which evidence is cited, and how checkably, and degrades where in a fused
list it appears.

\textbf{Does the backward edge pay for itself?} Conditionally. Removing it
costs $0.037$ F1 on HotpotQA at $p = 0.008$ and $0.001$ on 2WikiMultihopQA at
$p = 1.000$, while the loop fired on nearly half the questions of both and
doubled the cost of both. The counterweight is small, 2 and 5 false rejects,
so the answer selection is doing its job; the failure is the other way, 92 and
154 false accepts, and it is a property of a confidence signal with an AUC of
0.633 rather than of where the line was drawn. The edge helps on one dataset
and not the other, and this report states it that way.

\textbf{What does agency cost, and where does it fail?} About three times the
model calls of the strongest baseline and rather more in wall clock, for a
result that is worse on the answer string. It fails at composition, having
stopped failing at retrieval: decomposition and bridge-link errors largely
disappear against the ranker, and synthesis error at 0.228 and 0.236 replaces
them. The system finds the right sentences and extracts the wrong span.

\textbf{What actually improved it?} Not planning, not traversal, not a wider
evidence budget, not a larger model, and not voting. Deleting the Planner was
worth twelve F1 points on 2WikiMultihopQA at a third of the cost. Letting the
synthesiser reason, on the one-node plan, then took exact match from 0.432 to
0.524 on HotpotQA and from 0.224 to 0.456 on 2WikiMultihopQA, ties the
strongest baseline on the first and beats it by 0.192 on the second, with one
change and one mechanism. Every other hypothesis was dropped on its own
evidence, two of them before any GPU time was spent.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Sample size.} Results come from 250-question stratified samples. The
paired intervals are the right test and they decide significance, but nothing
here should be read as a precise effect size.

\textbf{The runs are not exactly reproducible.} Every interval in this report
is a bootstrap over questions, which answers what a different 250 would have
shown and is silent about what the same system does on the same questions run
again. With temperature 0 and a fixed seed the intended answer was nothing.

\input{../results/tables/replication.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:replication-drift} shows it is not nothing.
\texttt{agentic\_no\_verifier}, a configuration with no feedback loop to
blame, was re-run on the identical HotpotQA slice: exact match moved by 0.012,
F1 by 0.014, and 15 of 250 answers changed. The runs' own metadata records one
cause, an unpinned Python hash seed that let evidence pools assemble from sets
in varying order, and the harness now pins it. Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}
records the deeper one, which pinning does not remove: on a local inference
server with prefix caching, identical prompts at temperature zero produce
different completions, and on the calibration slice between 6 and 14 percent
of answers change between executions of byte-identical code. The consequence
is that an effect of a point or two is not established by a single execution.
The planner ablation and the kept configuration's gains are comfortably clear
of that floor; the graph's two nulls and the verifier's 2WikiMultihopQA null
are inside it and remain nulls. Every interval elsewhere is narrower than the
true uncertainty, because it holds the execution fixed.

\textbf{Model scale and domain.} Every result is conditional on a 4-bit 8B
model, and the improvement loop measured that a 14B model does not fit this
hardware at an acceptable throughput. Both benchmarks are Wikipedia: clean,
segmented and densely linked, which the entity graph exploits. Nothing here is
evidence of transfer to legal, biomedical or enterprise collections.

\textbf{The Verifier checks the sentence, not the answer.} The entailment
hypothesis is the synthesiser's answer sentence; the answer string the metrics
score is never entailed against anything, and the trace record in
Section~\ref{sec:errors} shows the two coming apart at a confidence of 0.82.
The correction, entailing a hypothesis built from the question and the answer
string, is straightforward and was not in place for any run reported here.

\textbf{An uncalibrated threshold.} The threshold of 0.55 was set a priori.
The post-hoc sweep over the calibration slice found the confidence blend to be
a usable ranking cut-point and an unusable probability, with an optimal
threshold whose bootstrap interval spans 0.45 to 0.64 and does not identify an
operating point. A genuine sweep needs one full run per candidate, since the
threshold changes what re-plans and therefore what is answered.

\textbf{Re-planning overwrites state, and the budget is cooperative.}
Sub-query identifiers restart at \texttt{q1} in every revision, so a re-plan
overwrites the previous cycle's results and the trace cannot fully reconstruct
what the synthesiser saw in cycle zero. The 300\,s budget is tested between
calls and cannot pre-empt one, which is how a question ran for 18.3 hours
inside a suspended call. Both are recorded defects, not applied to any run
here.

\textbf{Silent degradation.} If the entailment model cannot load, the Verifier
falls back to token containment, which scored 1.000 where the model scores
0.0008; every verdict becomes accept and the loop never fires. A preflight
check now refuses to start without the encoder. The general lesson outweighs
the bug: the fallbacks that keep a long run alive are exactly the ones that
quietly change what is measured, and every degradation path needs a gate or a
trace flag. The unassigned \texttt{truncated} field of
Section~\ref{sec:improvement} is the same lesson from the other side.

\section{Future Work}

\textbf{Synthesis, not planning.} The evidence points one way. The next unit
of effort belongs in answer extraction and in a verifier that can distinguish a
well-cited answer from a correct one: atomic claim decomposition, a hypothesis
that includes the answer string, and cross-source agreement, so the directive
tells the Planner what to look for rather than only that something is missing.

\textbf{Learned rather than heuristic decisions.} The routing table, the
rerank gate and the confidence blend are hand-specified functions whose inputs
are logged on every question. The cheap first step is offline: the trace
records the Planner's hint and the lexical features on every sub-query, so the
agreement between the hint that was honoured and the rule that would have fired
can be computed without a run, and it would say whether six of seven rules are
redundant or merely unreached.

\textbf{Evaluation on a caching inference server.} The reproducibility finding
generalises beyond this project. Any evaluation of this size on a local server
with prefix caching carries a noise floor that seed discipline does not remove,
and the honest protocol is several executions per configuration with the
spread reported, not one execution with a seed. That is a half day of compute
here and it is the first thing a continuation should do.

\textbf{Domain transfer.} Biomedical corpora have curated ontologies, so the
KG Navigator could traverse an authored graph and isolate how much of a graph's
value comes from structure rather than induction. Legal corpora invert the
challenge, with explicit citation networks but long, cross-referential
passages that would test alias linking to destruction.

\section{Toward a Thesis Extension}
\label{sec:thesis}

Two of the thesis directions in the brief follow from this system. The
self-reflective fact-checking track is the closer fit: the Verifier already
scores confidence and triggers further retrieval when it is low, and what it
lacks is multi-source cross-verification, provenance annotation and calibrated
uncertainty reported to the user. A multimodal setting needs a cross-modal
entailment model in place of the NLI stage and leaves the orchestration
intact. The query-decomposition track is the other: a spatio-temporal version
decomposes over archives indexed by time and location with the same validated
graph and feedback loop, and the cost model becomes the central design problem
once one tool invocation is a satellite-imagery request rather than a BM25
query. Both would be the natural place for the real-time external tools the
brief describes and this system deliberately omits.
