\section{Requirements for Evaluating an Agentic IR System}
\label{sec:data-requirements}

A dataset is not chosen because it is popular; it is chosen because it can
answer the questions a report has to ask. This system's central claim --- that
a validation agent can send useful feedback to a planning agent --- is only
meaningful if it can be falsified with numbers. Four requirements follow, and
they are stated before any dataset is named so that the selection reads as
derived rather than preferred.

\textbf{(a) Queries with relevance judgments.} Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation}
is required to report relevance metrics against baseline IR systems.
Recall@$k$, nDCG@10 and MRR are all defined over a mapping from queries to
relevant documents. A corpus without such a mapping --- a \emph{qrels} file, in
TREC terminology --- makes every one of those metrics undefined, not merely
inconvenient to compute. This requirement is therefore non-negotiable, and it
eliminates more candidate resources than the other three combined.

\textbf{(b) Genuinely compositional information needs.} The Planner exists to
decompose a question into a dependency graph of sub-questions. On a corpus of
single-fact look-ups the correct decomposition is the identity, every plan has
one node, and the entire agentic apparatus collapses onto the hybrid baseline
it is supposed to beat. Measuring a planner requires questions whose answers
are not contained in any single document.

\textbf{(c) Entity and structure annotations.} The KG Navigator traverses an
entity graph, and a traversal that cannot be scored is a description rather
than a result. Sentence-level supporting-fact annotations make retrieved
evidence checkable at the granularity the system actually emits; gold
relational triples make the traversal itself checkable.

\textbf{(d) A corpus that indexes and runs on consumer hardware.} Every
component of this system --- generation, embedding, reranking, entailment ---
runs locally on a single 8\,GB laptop GPU, with no paid API anywhere in the
project. A web-scale corpus is not merely expensive under that constraint; it
is unusable, and a resource that cannot be indexed cannot be evaluated.

BEIR~\cite{thakur2021beir} is the natural first place to look, and it satisfies
(a) and (d) comfortably across eighteen heterogeneous retrieval tasks. Most of
its tasks, however, express single-hop needs and carry no reasoning-structure
annotations, so they satisfy neither (b) nor (c). The two datasets adopted
below satisfy all four, and the first of them is itself one of BEIR's tasks,
which keeps this choice inside the standard IR evaluation canon rather than
outside it.

\section{On the Datasets Listed in the Assignment Brief}
\label{sec:table2}

Table~2 of the assignment brief lists five resources: The Pile, Common Crawl,
WikiText, OpenWebText and LAION-5B. All five are pretraining corpora. They
supply unlabelled text --- or, for LAION-5B, weakly labelled image--text pairs
--- at a scale suitable for fitting the parameters of a language or multimodal
model. None of them ships a query set, a relevance judgment, or a gold answer,
because none of them was assembled for that purpose. Requirement~(a) therefore
fails outright: with no query-to-document relevance mapping there is no
Recall@$k$ and no nDCG@10 to report, and the comparison against baseline IR
systems that Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation} is required to present is not
computable at all. This is not a deficiency in those corpora. It is a statement
about what they are for.

They are, in fact, exactly the right resources for a different layer of this
system. The reasoning component used throughout this project is a locally
served 8B-parameter language model, and its parametric knowledge --- its grasp
of English syntax, of world entities, of the shape of a well-formed JSON object
--- is the product of pretraining on corpora of precisely the kind Table~2
enumerates. The brief's list describes the substrate the agent brings to the
task; it does not describe the benchmark against which retrieval is scored.
Those are two different roles, and conflating them is what makes the
substitution below look like a departure when it is not one. The brief's own
instruction to ``always check for eventual updates of links and datasets via a
web search'' is the licence to select evaluation data on evaluation criteria,
and that is what Sections~\ref{sec:hotpotqa} and~\ref{sec:twowiki} do.

\section{HotpotQA}
\label{sec:hotpotqa}

HotpotQA~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa} is a multi-hop question answering dataset
built over English Wikipedia, in which each question is written so that
answering it requires combining facts from two distinct paragraphs. This work
uses the \texttt{distractor} setting, in which every question is accompanied by
its two gold paragraphs and eight retrieved distractors, and the
\texttt{validation} split, which contains 7{,}405 questions.

Its distinguishing property, and the reason it is the primary dataset here, is
the \emph{supporting facts} annotation: alongside the answer string, every
question carries a list of \texttt{(title, sent\_id)} pairs naming the
individual sentences a human annotator used. This is retrieval ground truth at
sentence granularity rather than merely answer ground truth, and it is what
makes the supporting-fact metrics \texttt{sp\_em} and \texttt{sp\_f1}
computable at all. It also fixes the granularity of the rest of the system:
evidence is carried as sentences, not passages, so that what the Verifier
reasons over and what the metrics score are the same objects.

Reading the distractor contexts of all 7{,}405 validation questions yields
73{,}700 paragraphs, which reduce to 66{,}581 distinct passages after the
deduplication described in Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}; 7{,}119 of the
discarded paragraphs were byte-identical repeats, and no title in this split
carries two different body texts. The corpus contains 269{,}602 sentences, a
mean of 4.049 sentences and 531.9 characters per passage. Of the 18{,}005 gold
supporting facts, 18{,}004 resolve against the built corpus. The single
exception is a genuine upstream annotation error rather than a preprocessing
artefact: question \texttt{5ae61bfd5542992663a4f261} cites sentence 902 of the
five-sentence paragraph ``Jimmy Butler (basketball)''. At a rate of
$5.6\times10^{-5}$ this is noise, but it is reported rather than silently
dropped, because the same counter is the tripwire that would catch a genuine
sentence-identifier drift.

One measured property of this split governs the sampling design in
Section~\ref{sec:sampling} and is easy to get wrong. HotpotQA labels questions
\texttt{easy}, \texttt{medium} or \texttt{hard}, but \textbf{all 7{,}405
validation questions carry \texttt{level=hard}} --- the easier levels exist
only in the training split. The informative partition here is the reasoning
type: 5{,}918 \texttt{bridge} questions, in which one entity must be resolved
before the second hop can even be posed, and 1{,}487 \texttt{comparison}
questions, in which two independent entities are retrieved and an attribute of
each is compared.

\section{2WikiMultihopQA}
\label{sec:twowiki}

2WikiMultihopQA~\cite{ho2020constructing} was constructed to expose the
reasoning steps a multi-hop question requires, rather than only its endpoints.
Its validation split contains 12{,}576 questions whose 125{,}760 context
paragraphs reduce to 54{,}957 distinct passages and 202{,}759 sentences, a mean
of 3.689 sentences and 427.2 characters per passage. Like HotpotQA it ships
sentence-level supporting facts --- 30{,}687 of them, all of which resolve.

The reason it is in this report is what it ships \emph{in addition}: gold
Wikidata evidence triples, 31{,}120 of them, covering all 12{,}576 questions.
These give the reasoning chain as structured relations rather than as prose,
and they convert the KG Navigator from a component this report describes into a
component this report measures. They also introduce the most severe leakage
risk in the project. Building the entity graph from those triples would let the
KG Navigator read the answer key, and every graph-attributed gain in
Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation} would be an artefact of that leak. The graph is
therefore built from corpus passage text only
(Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}); the gold triples are loaded exclusively by
the scoring code, and a test asserts that the graph builder never opens a file
whose name contains \texttt{evidence} or \texttt{triple}.

Unlike HotpotQA, all four of 2Wiki's reasoning types are populated:
\texttt{compositional} (5{,}236), \texttt{comparison} (3{,}040),
\texttt{bridge\_comparison} (2{,}751) and \texttt{inference} (1{,}549). The
third of these is the hardest case in either dataset, since it requires both a
bridge resolution and a comparison over its results.

This split is also textually messier than HotpotQA, in a way that bears
directly on ground truth. Because the same article appears in the contexts of
many questions, and because those contexts were detokenised inconsistently,
1{,}720 of the 54{,}957 titles ship two or more variants of the same article
(``Feo( c.\ 1471'' versus ``Feo (c.\ 1471''), producing 2{,}617 title
collisions. Since \texttt{sent\_id} indexes into a sentence list, the variant
retained decides whether a gold fact lands on the right sentence. The
resolution rule and its measured cost are given in
Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}.

\section{Preprocessing Pipeline}
\label{sec:preprocessing}

The pipeline is deliberately thin. Every transformation it applies is one that
cannot be undone downstream, and every transformation it declines to apply is
one a consumer can perform for itself.

\paragraph{Passage segmentation.} Both datasets already ship each context
paragraph as a list of sentences, and that list is adopted verbatim. Nothing in
this project calls a sentence splitter --- not NLTK, not PySBD, not a regular
expression. The reason is that supporting facts are \texttt{(title, sent\_id)}
pairs, so the sentence list \emph{is} the identifier space of the ground truth.
Re-splitting anywhere downstream would renumber every gold fact while
\texttt{sp\_em} and \texttt{sp\_f1} continued to produce entirely plausible
numbers --- a silent failure, which is the worst kind. The split is performed
once, at corpus build time, and persisted. Empty sentences keep their slot,
because the slot is the identifier.

\paragraph{Canonical identifiers.} Each passage is keyed by
\texttt{doc\_id} $=$ \texttt{\{source\}:\{quote(title)\}}, for example
\texttt{hotpotqa:Accolade\%20Wines}. The identifier is URL-safe, stable across
rebuilds, and derived from the title alone, so that a passage returned by any
of the three scorers, an entity node in the graph, and a gold supporting fact
all name the same object in the same way.

\paragraph{Deduplication.} The distractor setting repeats paragraphs across
questions, so the raw paragraph stream is roughly an order of magnitude larger
than the distinct corpus. HotpotQA collapses cleanly: 7{,}119 byte-identical
repeats removed, zero collisions. 2Wiki removes 68{,}186 identical repeats but
leaves 2{,}617 cases in which one title carries two different texts. The rule
is to keep the variant with the \emph{most sentences} --- the widest identifier
space --- with ties going to the first occurrence. The rule is decided without
consulting any gold data, and its cost is measured: over 2Wiki's 30{,}687 gold
facts, keeping the first occurrence would leave 70 identifiers out of range and
211 pointing at the wrong sentence, whereas keeping the widest leaves 0 out of
range and 150, or 0.489\%, landing on a slightly different detokenisation of
the intended sentence. Both residuals are counted in the build statistics
rather than rounded away.

\paragraph{Normalisation, and what is deliberately not done.} The only
transformation applied to corpus text is the collapse of whitespace runs inside
a sentence, which is necessary because HotpotQA prefixes continuation sentences
with a space and 2Wiki does not. There is no lowercasing, no stopword removal,
no stemming and no punctuation stripping at the corpus level. Those are
properties of a \emph{scorer's analyser}, not of a corpus: BM25 applies
Snowball stemming and an English stopword list inside its own analyser, whereas
the dense encoder needs original case and punctuation to distinguish proper
nouns, and the NLI premise encoder needs well-formed sentences. Baking any of
those steps into the stored text would be irreversible and would degrade two of
the three scorers in order to help one. Conversely, one convention \emph{is}
shared across all of them: every scorer sees the same title-prefixed unit,
\texttt{"Title. body"}. A channel that indexes titles, fused with a channel
that does not, produces rankings that are not comparable, and rank fusion would
absorb the mismatch silently rather than expose it.

\paragraph{Entity extraction and alias resolution.} Graph nodes are passage
titles, normalised by NFKD decomposition, lowercasing, diacritic stripping,
removal of parenthetical disambiguators and whitespace collapse. A node's alias
set is its surface title plus the no-parenthetical variant plus the
comma-inverted variant, and the aliases are compiled into a trie for
longest-match linking at query time. No named-entity recogniser and no language
model is involved, so linking is deterministic and costs nothing at run time.

\paragraph{Entity graph construction.} A directed edge $A \to B$ is created
when an alias of $B$ occurs in the text of passage $A$. Each edge stores the
\texttt{doc\_id}, the \texttt{sent\_id} and the containing sentence, which is
what makes a traversal result citable evidence at the same granularity as a
retrieved passage rather than an unscoreable decoration. The relation label is
the shortest verb-anchored token window between the two mentions, truncated to
six tokens and defaulting to \texttt{mentions}; there is no parser dependency
and no generation step. Edge weight is $1/(1 + \log(1 + \mathrm{outdeg}(A)))$,
so hub pages contribute weak edges. As recorded in
\texttt{data/indexes/\allowbreak\{dataset\}\_kg\_meta.json}, the HotpotQA graph
retains 64{,}733 nodes and 123{,}759 edges from 417{,}881 candidates and builds
in 49.4\,s; the 2Wiki graph retains 52{,}959 nodes and 63{,}073 edges from
161{,}572 candidates and builds in 32.9\,s. Pruning is dominated by a
document-frequency cap on mentions, which removed 446 and 172 hub nodes
respectively: strings such as \texttt{one} and \texttt{united states} are
legitimate page titles, but their occurrences are not relational assertions,
and without the cap the graph collapses into a hairball centred on them.
Verb-anchored relation labels are recovered for 76.8\% of HotpotQA edges and
79.0\% of 2Wiki edges, and Section~\ref{sec:kg-traversal} is explicit about
what the remainder costs.

\paragraph{Indexing.} Each corpus is indexed twice: a BM25 index with Snowball
stemming, and an exact inner-product FAISS index over 384-dimensional
\texttt{bge-small-en-v1.5} embeddings. Together these occupy 129.1\,MB for
HotpotQA and 103.6\,MB for 2Wiki --- small enough to hold in memory, which is
why exact search is used in place of an approximate index and why no recall
loss is introduced at the index level. The 2Wiki build took 4.1\,s for BM25 and
97.6\,s for the dense index on GPU.

\section{Evaluation Sampling}
\label{sec:sampling}

Evaluation is run on a frozen 250-question sample per dataset rather than on
the full split, and the reason is arithmetic. Local generation runs at a
measured 52 tokens per second warm on an RTX 5060 with 8\,GB of VRAM, giving a
derived rather than measured planner call of about 3.75\,s. The pre-run
estimate on which the sampling decision was taken put a full agentic question
at roughly 22--25\,s once retrieval, reranking and entailment were included;
the medians actually measured, reported in
Table~\ref{tab:agent-metrics-hotpotqa} and Table~\ref{tab:agent-metrics-twowiki},
came out higher, and the estimate is recorded here as the estimate it was
rather than revised to match. On that estimate one agentic configuration over
HotpotQA's full 7{,}405-question validation split is on the order of 45 hours,
and the evaluation grid comprises nine configurations across two datasets. At
250 questions the same grid was estimated at 12--16 hours, which is feasible
on this hardware; at 7{,}405 it is not.

The sample is drawn once, by \texttt{scripts/sample\_eval\_set.py} with
seed~42, materialised to
\texttt{data/processed/\allowbreak\{dataset\}\_eval\_250.jsonl}, and never
re-sampled at
run time. Its SHA-256 is recorded in every run's \texttt{meta.json}, so a
silently changed sample is detectable after the fact.

The stratification variable is question type, not difficulty, and that is a
measured decision rather than a stylistic one. As reported in
Section~\ref{sec:hotpotqa}, all 7{,}405 HotpotQA validation questions are
labelled \texttt{hard}. Stratifying on \texttt{level} would therefore produce a
single stratum --- an unstratified sample presented as a stratified one, with
the label doing no work while implying that it does. Stratifying on
\texttt{type} preserves the $0.799/0.201$ bridge--comparison ratio exactly, at
200 and 50 questions. For 2Wiki the four reasoning types are preserved at 104
compositional, 60 comparison, 55 bridge-comparison and 31 inference.
Table~\ref{tab:dataset-sample} gives the full composition against the split.

Two hundred and fifty questions is enough to separate the systems compared
here. In the worst case --- a metric near 0.5, treated as an unpaired binomial
proportion --- the 95\% normal-approximation half-width is
$1.96\sqrt{0.25/250} \approx 0.062$, an interval of roughly $\pm6.2$ points.
The comparisons that matter in Chapter~\ref{ch:implementation} are paired,
however: every configuration answers the identical 250 questions, so the
bootstrap is taken over per-question \emph{differences} (1{,}000 resamples,
\texttt{evaluation.bootstrap\_samples}), and the error correlation between
systems sharing a corpus, an index and a question set makes those intervals
materially tighter than the unpaired figure suggests; the realised widths are
reported with the results rather than assumed here. The three component
ablations run on the same 250 questions as every other configuration, not on
a subsample, so that every ablation delta is a paired comparison over an
identical question set; where an ablation run had not yet covered all 250
questions the generated table marks the row as preliminary and withholds its
delta rather than pairing a prefix against the full slice.

Each dataset also gets a 50-question calibration slice,
\texttt{\{dataset\}\_calib\_50.jsonl}, written beside the evaluation file in
\texttt{data/processed/} and verified to have zero question-identifier overlap
with the evaluation 250. Its intended purpose was to fix
the Verifier's \texttt{confidence\_threshold} --- 0.55 --- before the
evaluation run began. That threshold controls the re-plan rate, which is
itself one of the headline agent-specific measures, so sweeping it on the
evaluation set would be tuning on the test set and would invalidate the very
metric it was tuned to improve. In the event the sweep was run over this slice
only after the evaluation, on confidences produced at the configured value,
and Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusions} reports what such a post-hoc sweep can and
cannot establish; the slice's disjointness is what keeps even that diagnostic
reading free of the evaluation questions. Table~\ref{tab:dataset-corpus}
summarises the two corpora as built.
