\begin{abstract}
Most retrieval-augmented systems are pipelines: they retrieve once and generate
once. That structure cannot answer a question whose evidence is distributed
across documents, because no single document is rankable as the answer. This
report presents an agentic alternative --- four agents coordinated by an
explicit state machine, covering all five tasks of the assignment brief. A
Planner decomposes a question into a validated graph of dependent sub-queries; a
Retrieval Agent routes each sub-query to BM25, dense, or hybrid search via a
seven-rule decision table costing zero model calls; a Knowledge Graph Navigator
traverses an entity graph induced from corpus text to recover bridge entities;
and a Verifier scores the answer against its own citations using entailment and
citation grounding. The central contribution is one backward edge: below its
confidence threshold the Verifier returns a re-plan directive to the Planner
rather than an answer. That edge is the difference between a pipeline and an
agent, and it is isolated by ablation rather than asserted. The system is
evaluated on HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA against lexical, dense, hybrid, and
agentic baselines, reporting agent-specific cost alongside answer quality.
Everything runs locally on one consumer GPU with no paid API.
The result is not the one the architecture was hoping for and is reported as
measured. On answer accuracy the agentic system does not lead: exact match is
0.432 on HotpotQA and 0.224 on 2WikiMultihopQA against 0.504 and 0.264 for a
self-ask baseline that decomposes but never verifies, a deficit that is
significant on HotpotQA under the paired bootstrap and not on 2WikiMultihopQA.
The backward edge does measurable work on one of the two datasets and none on
the other: removing it, with everything else held fixed, changes HotpotQA F1
by $-0.037$ $[-0.065, -0.011]$, a significant loss, and 2WikiMultihopQA F1 by
$-0.001$ $[-0.025, +0.025]$, $p = 1.000$ --- though it fired on 98 of those
250 questions and charged the same doubled cost. The contribution is therefore
reported as conditional rather than general. On attribution the agentic system is not more accurate but
differently accountable. Its supporting-fact F1 of 0.481 and 0.421 sits above
the strongest retrieval baseline's 0.412 and 0.327, though the two are scored
under different protocols and the tables place no significance mark between
them; it is scored
on the sentences it actually cited, at a citation grounding of 0.960 and 0.976
where every baseline resolves no citation at all; the generative baselines,
scored on their whole evidence pool, reach a supporting-fact F1 that is not
comparable with it and is marked as such, while on the like-for-like recall
of the whole pool the agentic system leads every baseline, 0.793 and 0.684
against 0.706 and 0.622, significantly on both. The gated verifier
adjudication and the deterministic extraction ladder displaced 2.60 and 3.10
model calls per question that the system would
otherwise have made --- though in the headline run the Planner's own tool hint
pre-empted the routing table on every sub-query, so six of its seven rules
never executed --- and the re-plan loop fired on 47.2\% and 39.2\% of
questions. The defensible claim is about grounded, auditable answers rather
than raw accuracy, and the cost of that guarantee is reported beside it.
\end{abstract}
